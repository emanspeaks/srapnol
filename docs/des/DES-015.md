# Reduced-Hessian factor

With basic \(B\), superbasic \(S\), and nonbasic \(N\) columns of \([J \; {-I}]\), any
step keeping the equalities is \(p = Z p_S\) with \(Z = [-B^{-1}S;\ I;\ 0]\). \(Z\) is
never formed: \(Zv\) and \(Z'w\) cost one solve with \(B\) (or \(B'\)) plus a sparse
product with \(S\). The reduced Hessian \(Z'HZ\) is held as a dense upper-triangular
Cholesky factor \(R\) of dimension \(n_S\): a column is added through an \(R'\) solve,
deleted through a sweep of Givens rotations, and the BFGS change applied as a rank-one
update via rotations. \(R\) is kept positive definite by diagonal modification when an
update fails, and its condition estimate is monitored. This is the SQOPT design and
why `superbasics_limit` exists.
